04 / What we want to know

Four questions to guide the data.

OBJECTIVE

Explore how changes in memory-task performance and response times differ across the four experimental groups.

Why this dataset? It lets us test the popular idea of “brain rot” with evidence — without assuming permanent brain damage.

What did the data actually say?

11 / Conclusion

The break didn't change how we read words.
It changed whether we remembered what we meant to do.
What we learned

Prospective memory is far more sensitive to interruptions than word recognition, and the TikTok group showed the clearest average decline. The type of break seems to matter.

What we can't conclude

One experiment, exploratory analysis. It doesn't prove TikTok causes memory loss, show lasting effects, or replace the study's statistical tests.

Key takeaway: if you need to remember to do something after a break, the kind of break you take may matter.
